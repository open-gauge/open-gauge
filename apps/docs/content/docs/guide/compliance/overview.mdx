Open Gauge is built to be usable as the system of record behind an accredited or certified
quality/metrology programme, not just a database of sensors. This section documents, standard by
standard, exactly which clauses Open Gauge's features address today, which are partially
addressed, and which remain the responsibility of the operating organization rather than the
software.

## How to read these pages

Each standard gets its own page with three parts:

1. **Summary** — publisher, edition, scope, and why it's relevant to a self-hosted calibration
   platform.
2. **Compliance table** — one row per relevant clause, with a status and a pointer to where in
   Open Gauge that clause is addressed.
3. **Detail** — one section per clause, with a short excerpt of the requirement and an explanation
   of how Open Gauge addresses it (or the concrete gap, if it doesn't yet).

### Status legend

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ Met | The feature exists today and satisfies the clause as written. |
| ⚠️ Partial | Open Gauge provides part of what the clause requires, but a real gap remains — described in the clause's detail section. |
| ❌ Not met | Open Gauge does not currently address this clause. |
| ➖ N/A | The clause governs something outside what a software system can satisfy on its own (see below). |

### Scope: what these pages do and don't cover

Certification and accreditation standards mix two kinds of requirements: things a **software
system** can implement (traceability chains, uncertainty calculation, record immutability, report
content, access control), and things that are inherently an **organizational/process** matter —
personnel competence records, impartiality policy, internal audit programmes, facilities and
environmental control, complaint handling. Open Gauge's compliance tables only include clauses of
the first kind; the organizational clauses are real requirements a certified lab must still
satisfy, just not ones a documentation page about software can meaningfully mark "met."

### Quoting policy

Where a clause's exact wording matters, these pages quote it — briefly, one clause at a time, for
the purpose of explaining how Open Gauge addresses it. Several of the standards referenced here
(the ISO and SAE/IAQG documents) are commercially licensed, copyrighted publications; these pages
do not reproduce them beyond what's needed to ground each explanation. For the authoritative full
text, obtain a licensed copy from the publisher (ISO, SAE International, etc.) — publicly
available documents (JCGM/BIPM guides, EA publications, US federal regulations) are the exception.

### This is not a certification

These pages describe the software's capabilities as implemented and verified against the current
codebase at the date noted on each page. They are engineering documentation, not a legal opinion,
an accreditation, or a guarantee that any specific deployment is compliant — actual conformance
depends on how an organization configures and operates Open Gauge, and on the rest of its quality
system.
