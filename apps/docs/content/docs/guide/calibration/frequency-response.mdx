---
title: Frequency response
description: Calibrating sensors that deliver a signal in the frequency domain — accelerometers, microphones, and similar — as a sensitivity sweep instead of a fitted curve.
---

Some sensors don't have a single reference-vs-measured relationship the way a pressure or
temperature sensor does — an accelerometer or a microphone's output depends on the *frequency* at
which it's excited as well as the amplitude. **Frequency response** is Step 2's fourth
**Input data method**, alongside
[Reference vs Measured, Reference vs Indicated, and Model](./coefficients-only.mdx): instead of
fitting a curve, you tune a platform and the sensor together at several frequencies and record the
reference standard's and the sensor's output amplitude at each one. It's offered for every
**Calibration purpose**, including After Repair — there's no as-found/as-left split for this mode.

## Sweep entry (Step 2) [#sweep-entry]

Choosing **Frequency response** replaces the usual point table with a settings row and a sweep
table:

- **Frequency unit** — Hz, kHz, MHz, or GHz, applied to every row.
- **Reference unit** — the reference standard's physical quantity unit (e.g. g, Pa), from the
  channel's physical quantity.
- **Measured unit** — the sensor's own output unit (e.g. mV, V), from the channel's output signal
  type.
- **Amplitude type** — dB, RMS, Peak-to-Peak, or Peak, applied to both the reference and measured
  amplitude at every frequency (both must be read the same way for the sensitivity ratio below to
  mean anything).
- **Include phase offset** — off by default. When enabled, an extra Offset column appears in the
  table and an angle unit (° or rad) becomes selectable — use it if your reference/measurement
  setup also gives you the phase difference between the reference and the sensor's output at each
  frequency.

Below that, the same manual-entry-or-CSV-upload table every other mode uses, with one row per
frequency point: **Frequency**, **Reference**, **Measured**, and **Offset** (only shown when
enabled). At least 2 rows are required. A CSV upload expects a header row with columns named
`Frequency`, `Reference`, `Measured`, and optionally `Offset`.

## Sensitivity model (Step 3) [#sensitivity]

There's no curve to fit here — Step 3 shows one panel instead of the usual
Method/[Uncertainty](./uncertainty-budget.mdx)/[Conformity](./decision-rules.mdx) stack:

- **Baseline frequency** [#baseline-frequency] — a dropdown listing every frequency entered on
  Step 2. Pick the one your sensor's output is normally specified against (often the sensor's
  rated frequency, e.g. 100 Hz for many accelerometers).
- **Sensitivity** — the ratio between the measured and reference amplitudes *at the baseline
  frequency* (measured ÷ reference), in output unit per physical-quantity unit (e.g. mV/g). This
  is the calibration's saved result.

Every other frequency's sensitivity is expressed as a **% deviation** from the baseline: for a
point with sensitivity *s* and the baseline's sensitivity *g*, deviation = (*s* − *g*) / *g* × 100.
The baseline point's own deviation is always exactly 0.

Two charts, both with round markers connected by lines, an x-axis that switches to logarithmic
automatically when the sweep spans a decade or more (linear otherwise), and hover tooltips:

- **Sensitivity vs. frequency** — always shown. Hovering a point shows its frequency, sensitivity,
  and % deviation from the baseline.
- **Phase vs. frequency** — shown only when **Include phase offset** is enabled, stacked below the
  sensitivity chart. Only points with an entered offset are plotted.

Since there's no uncertainty budget or decision rule for this mode, a Frequency response
calibration is never marked as failing conformity — there's no specification to compare against.
The saved record still goes through the normal [approval workflow](./overview.mdx#approval-workflow)
if a **Checked by** reviewer was assigned, and downloads a certificate like any other calibration.

## Internal representation

Open Gauge stores every calibration's model the same way regardless of mode, so a Frequency
response result is represented internally as a polynomial of order 1 with no offset — a pure gain:
`poly_order = 1`, `poly_coefficients = [gain, 0]` (highest degree first, the same convention every
other fitted model uses). No regression is actually run; the "fit" is just the baseline frequency's
own measured/reference ratio. The full sweep — every frequency's reference/measured amplitude,
optional offset, and computed sensitivity/deviation — is stored separately, one row per point, and
is what powers the two charts above.
